The research behind the fly

Google’s connectomics work

Google Research’s Connectomics team develops AI that reconstructs neurons from electron-microscope images. Its flood-filling networks trace cells through image volumes, helping turn microscopic sections into three-dimensional wiring maps. Researchers Michał Januszewski and Viren Jain describe this work as a long-running collaboration with HHMI Janelia.

For the male fruit fly central nervous system, Google contributed reconstruction technology to a project led by HHMI Janelia Research Campus. Janelia’s experts verified and annotated the map. Google also created Neuroglancer, an open-source viewer for exploring large scientific datasets.

The resulting MaleCNS resource maps over 166,000 neurons and 125 million synapses across the brain and ventral nerve cord. These maps describe biological wiring; they provide a foundation for computational experiments.

Read Google’s research account · Explore HHMI Janelia’s MaleCNS resource

What runs here

This site uses measured MaleCNS neuron positions and a connectome-based spiking simulation. The browser implementation and fly assets come from Xenova’s fruit-fly-simulation repository. The simulation approach builds on Shiu and colleagues’ computational brain model.

Google’s AI helped reconstruct biological wiring. The token response rules and expressive fly movements are our application logic. They are not a trading model trained by Google, and this website is independent of the research teams.

Choose a room

Each address has a shareable page and a separate transaction stream. Indexed V2, V3 and Uniswap V4 pools are supported; bonding curves before migration and other chains are not yet supported.

How the fly responds

  • Buys: attractant or reward inputs; climbing, upward darts, orbits, hops and wing flutters. Directly stimulated cells glow green.
  • Sells: deterrent inputs; descents, swerves, spirals, recoil and shaking. Larger or clustered falling sells can activate looming inputs. Directly stimulated cells glow red.
  • Direction and intensity: trade size adjusts stimulation strength. Either side can turn left or right. During bursts, reactions overlap while the transaction list retains the individual swaps.
  • Quiet periods: the fly settles and grooms. Mood labels describe animation, not measured emotions. Pink is reserved for fee inputs; fee events are not currently decoded.

Read the live view

The server subscribes to indexed pools, waits two blocks, and pushes updates to your browser. Overlapping scans recover missed logs. This is near-real-time observation, not finality or a guarantee of every trade. USD sizes are estimates. The status beside Transactions distinguishes a quiet market from a delayed connection.

Drag either view to orbit and scroll to zoom. Follow tracks the fly; Side and Top change the camera. Connections shows a sparse sample of measured edges. Brain + nerve cord expands the anatomy. The background palette separates space visually; it does not label anatomical regions or trade direction.
